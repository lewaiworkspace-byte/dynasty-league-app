# EL DYNASTY FUTBOL LEAGUE-O

## EDFL RULE BOOK

**Version 2.1 — September 21, 2026**

---

### About this book

This book states the rules of play of El Dynasty Futbol League-o. It is the
league's governing document. It is complete: every rule that binds an owner is
written here, and nothing that binds an owner is written anywhere else.

This book describes no software. It names no application, no platform, no
service and no vendor, and it does not depend on any. A league that adopted
these rules and ran them on paper would be running them correctly. Where a rule
needs a fact that some system must supply — a player's statistics, his position,
his injury status — this book names the *role* that supplies it and leaves the
choice of provider to the commissioner under 1.13.

Three documents serve this league and each has one job:

| Document | Answers |
|---|---|
| **Rule Book** (this book) | What are the rules? |
| **Technical Manual** | How does the league application work, and how does it enforce these rules? |
| **Owner How-To Manual** | How do I do a thing in the application? |

The Rule Book governs. Where the Technical Manual or the How-To Manual differs
from this book, this book is the rule and the other document is the defect.

### Numbering, and why it has not changed

Section and clause numbers in this book are the numbers this league has cited
since Version 16, and they are unchanged. A reference to 5.17 means what it has
always meant. Numbers are never reused for different content; a repealed or
struck rule keeps its number and is marked repealed, so that older citations
resolve rather than mislead.

A clause is cited as **RB 5.17(c)** where the document must be distinguished,
and as **5.17(c)** inside this book.

### How to read a rule

- **"Must", "shall" and "may not"** state an obligation or a prohibition.
- **"May"** states a permission.
- **Times** are United States Eastern Time unless a rule says otherwise.
- **Money** is EDFL money and is not real money, except in Section 2, which is
  about dues and prizes, and in 2.1(c), which converts real money into EDFL
  money.
- **Defined terms** are listed in Section 11 and carry their defined meaning
  wherever they appear.
- **Transitional rules** — rules that apply to one named season only — are in
  Schedule A and are struck when they expire. A rule in the body is permanent
  unless Schedule A says otherwise for a stated season.
- **Matters reserved** — questions the commissioner has not yet ruled on — are
  in Schedule B. Nothing in Schedule B is a rule.

# 1. GENERAL

## 1.1 Intent and loopholes

(a) These rules are to be read for their evident intent. An owner may not take
an advantage by reading a rule against its obvious purpose, by exploiting a
drafting gap, or by asserting that conduct the rules plainly do not contemplate
is permitted because they do not forbid it.

(b) A rule that is silent is not a permission. Where the rules are silent, 1.3
applies.

## 1.2 Officers

(a) **Commissioner.** The league has one commissioner, who holds every authority
these rules give to an officer and every authority they do not give to another.

(b) **Co-commissioner.** The league has one co-commissioner. He may act as
commissioner for a time-critical decision when the commissioner is unavailable.
He holds the commissioner's authority for any decision concerning the
commissioner's own team that is not a league-wide or league-majority decision.
He has access to all league documents and may make routine edits to them. He may
not exercise a power Appendix A.2 reserves to the commissioner.

(c) **Treasurer.** The treasurer collects all dues before the start of the league
year and pays prize money as soon as practicable after the EDFL Bowl.

(d) **Approvers.** The league may appoint two approvers, who may approve a trade
or other action involving a minority of owners where both the commissioner and
the co-commissioner have a conflict of interest. The offices are presently
vacant.

(e) **League document editors.** The commissioner and one other person the
commissioner appoints may edit operational league documents — contract records,
draft records and similar. This authority does not extend to these rules.

(f) **Appointment and removal.** The commissioner appoints and removes the
co-commissioner, the treasurer, the approvers and the document editors. Every
appointment and removal is published under 1.11.

## 1.3 Grievances

(a) A grievance is brought to the commissioner, who decides it within 24 hours
where that is possible.

(b) If a grievance remains after the commissioner's decision, the aggrieved
owner, any other owner or the commissioner proposes a solution. All owners vote.
The solution taking a majority is adopted. If no solution takes a majority, the
two highest-polling solutions go to a run-off. If the run-off ties, the aggrieved
owner chooses between the tied solutions.

(c) The grievance process is for a situation these rules do not cover. It is not
an appeal against a rule that does cover the situation.

(d) An owner has no grievance under this section for having acted on a statement
published under 7.9, whether or not it was true.

## 1.4 The season and the weekly cycle

(a) The league year begins on March 1st and ends on the day of the EDFL Bowl.

(b) The **Off-Season** runs from March 1st through the Sunday before the first
game of the NFL season.

(c) The **In-Season** runs from the Monday before the first game of the NFL
season through the day of the EDFL Bowl.

(d) The **Dead Season** runs from the day after the EDFL Bowl through the last
day of February.

(e) *Reserved.* This letter carried the 2026 In-Season transition, which is now
in Schedule A.1. The letter is held open so that citations to it resolve.

(f) **The weekly transaction cycle.** In season, each week turns on four fixed
instants. They are fixed per week on the League Calendar and are never computed
from the day of the week, because the NFL moves games.

| Instant | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Tuesday 00:00** | Weekly salary is charged for the week about to be played. The waiver wire opens, carrying every player cut since the previous Tuesday 00:00. Elevated practice squad players return to the practice squad, unless held under 3.3(d)(i). |
| **Wednesday 00:00** | The waiver wire is adjudicated. Unclaimed players clear to free agency. |
| **Thursday 00:00** | Every roster must be compliant. |
| **End of the week's last game** | Cuts designated for the end of the week take effect. |

(i) Where a week's first game falls on a Wednesday, that week's compliance
instant is **16:00 Wednesday**, before kickoff.

(ii) Salary is charged **in advance of the week it covers**. A player cut before
Tuesday 00:00 does not cost his team that week.

(iii) The four instants of a week are fixed before that week begins and are not
altered once fixed.

## 1.5 The League Calendar

(a) The league maintains a calendar of every dated instant these rules produce,
stated in Eastern Time, each entry citing the rule it comes from.

(b) The calendar is the authoritative record of *when*. A change of date is a
change to the calendar.

(c) The rule is the authoritative record of *what*. Where a calendar entry and
the rule it cites disagree, the rule governs and the calendar entry is corrected.

(d) Only the commissioner may set or alter a calendar entry.

## 1.6 In-Season rule changes

(a) A rule change during the In-Season requires the approval of every owner. An
owner who abstains counts as a yes, and where the change offers several options,
an abstention counts for the commissioner's choice.

(b) An in-season rule vote may be initiated only by the commissioner or by a
group of six owners.

(c) Each team has one vote.

## 1.7 Off-Season rule changes

(a) At least one rules vote is held each league year, during the Dead Season.
Rule change suggestions are due to the commissioner by **February 10th**; the
league votes on accepted proposals **February 14th through February 20th**,
concluding before the League Reset Period.

(b) The commissioner may call an additional vote at any point in the Off-Season.

(c) A rule takes effect on a majority unless the vote language requires more.

(d) **Commissioner-directed proposals** take effect unless blocked through the
grievance process. They are administrative in nature and serve league
management.

(e) **Commissioner-accepted proposals** go to the ballot.

(f) **Unaccepted proposals.** A proposal the commissioner does not accept reaches
the ballot if the proposing owner obtains the support of three other owners
before the proposal deadline. Where the deadline has passed or no vote is near,
the support of five owners forces a special rules vote.

(g) Each team has one vote. An owner must be paid in full under 2.1(b) to vote.

## 1.8 Teams

(a) The league has ten teams. Expansion to twelve is an aim, not a rule.

(b) A team name changes only with the commissioner's approval or on a change of
ownership.

(c) Each team carries a three-letter code, assigned by the commissioner, used
wherever these rules break a tie alphabetically.

## 1.9 Rounding

All money, real and EDFL, is stated in whole dollars. A calculation yielding a
fraction of a dollar is rounded **up** to the next whole dollar, unless a rule
states otherwise.

## 1.10 Correspondence and publication

(a) **Chat.** Every owner maintains a presence in the league's official business
chat.

(b) **Email.** Every owner gives the commissioner an email address he checks at
least daily. An email is deemed read 24 hours after it is sent unless the
recipient says otherwise.

(c) **The wires.** The league publishes three automatic notice streams. None of
them is an official record of league action; the Action Log under 1.11 and the
league's own records are.

(i) **The transaction wire** publishes every entry of the league transaction
log, roster moves included, and names the losing bidders of a contested free
agency window once it has resolved.

(ii) **The league office wire** announces every League Calendar entry three
times — seven days before, one day before, and at the hour, the last omitted
where the entry's time is not exact; publishes every fine with the team, the
amount and the reason in full; and carries notices the commissioner or
co-commissioner writes. A league office notice is published to the league, never
privately to one owner.

(iii) **The rumour wire** publishes what owners submit to the rumour desk under
7.9(c).

(iv) A wire never addresses an owner personally, and nothing an owner can do
makes a wire speak.

## 1.11 Transparency and the Action Log

(a) Every administrative action an officer takes is recorded automatically in a
public Action Log, with the time it happened and the reason given at the time.
The log is readable by anyone, including a person who is not an owner.

(b) **Deletion is for correction only.** Removing a record is for correcting a
mistake — a contract entered with wrong figures, or test data. It is never a
substitute for cutting a player, which is a real league action producing dead
money under 5.18. Every deletion requires a stated reason, and a full copy of
what was removed is preserved inside the log entry.

(c) **Published auction results are final.** Once an auction tier has been
verified under 6.1(g), no bid or result from it may be deleted.

## 1.12 League year rollover

(a) The league year advances on **March 1st**, one season at a time. The
commissioner or co-commissioner performs the rollover.

(b) **Contracts end.** Every contract whose last real season has passed expires.
The player becomes uncontracted and his roster place is released.

(c) **Void proration comes due.** Proration still outstanding on an expiring
contract is charged to the new league year under 5.10(c).

(d) **Expiry is not a transaction.** It consumes no June 1st designation and
requires no counterparty.

(e) **Roster limits do not apply.** The active roster maximum and minimum in 3.6
are not enforced in the Off-Season. A team is expected to fall below the minimum
at rollover and to rebuild.

(f) **Reversal.** The commissioner or co-commissioner may reverse the most recent
rollover, with a stated reason and no time limit, until the new league year has
been built on — that is, until a contract has been signed, a player cut, a trade
made, a contract restructured, or an auction tier created in it.

(g) This section is not the cap rollover in 5.5, which is a separate mechanic
carrying a team's unused cap into its next Salary Ceiling.

## 1.13 Designated providers

(a) The commissioner designates, and publishes on the League Calendar, the
provider of each of the following. A designation stands until it is changed and
published.

(i) **The statistics provider** — the source of official weekly player
statistics, from which scoring under 8.5 is computed.

(ii) **The position authority** — the source that determines which position a
player is eligible to fill under 3.1 and 3.5.

(iii) **The injury authority** — the source of the injury designations named in
3.4(b).

(iv) **The prospect source** — the source of the published grades and ranks of
the coming rookie class under 7.9(e).

(b) **The league's own record governs.** Contracts, the salary cap, Owner Cash,
roster status, transactions and every figure these rules compute are held in the
league's own records. Where a designated provider's record of a roster or a
transaction differs from the league's own record, the league's record governs and
the provider's is corrected.

(c) A designated provider supplies facts. It never decides a rule.

# 2. FEES AND PRIZES

## 2.1 Fees

(a) Dues are **\$150** a year. All of it is prize money.

(b) Dues are paid in full by the start of the league year, March 1st. An owner
who has paid remains paid in full until the start of the next league year.

(i) An owner must be paid in full to vote.

(ii) An owner who has not paid in full has his team placed on **auto-drive**.
An auto-drive team is managed by the commissioner and co-commissioner, who make
no transaction except where one is necessary to field a full roster, and who
then make only minimum acquisitions of the best available player at the position
needed.

(iii) An owner who has not paid by the end of Week 2 of the regular season may
be removed from the league at the commissioner's discretion.

(iv) The commissioner may offer a financial incentive to attract a new owner.

(c) **Purchasing Owner Cash.** An owner may buy additional Owner Cash at **\$10
of real money per \$250 of EDFL Cash**. The real money is added to that season's
prize pool on top of dues and is paid **75% to the champion and 25% to the
runner-up**, each rounded to the nearest whole dollar.

## 2.2 Prizes

(a) **Payment.** All prizes, weekly prizes included, are paid as soon as
practicable after the EDFL Bowl.

(b) **Prize money goes to dues first.** Before any prize is paid, the following
season's dues are held from it and applied to those dues.

(i) An owner winning \$140 receives nothing and owes \$10 the following season.

(ii) An owner winning \$310 is paid \$160 and owes nothing the following season.

(c) **Weekly prize.** The highest-scoring team in each week of the regular season
receives **\$25**.

(d) **Year-end prizes.**

(i) EDFL Bowl champion (1st place) — **\$500**, together with the Player's Cup.

(ii) EDFL Bowl runner-up (2nd place) — **\$200**.

(iii) Consolation game winner (3rd place) — **\$100**.

(iv) Playoff bonus, to each of the six teams that qualify — **\$50**.

(v) Coach of the Year, most points for over the regular season — **\$25**.

(vi) Mr. Unlucky, most points against over the regular season — **\$25**.

## 2.3 Monetization

If the league is ever scaled and monetized, an original owner still in the league
at that time is entitled to up to **3%** equity by virtue of being an original
owner.

# 3. ROSTER

A team's players sit in one of three groups: the **Active Roster**, the
**practice squad**, and **injured reserve**. Only the Active Roster scores.

## 3.1 Starting lineup

(a) The nominal starting lineup is twelve places: **1 QB, 2 RB, 4 WR, 2 TE,
2 FLEX (RB, WR or TE), 1 K**.

(b) Eligibility to fill a position is determined by the position authority
designated under 1.13(a)(ii).

(c) No lineup is set. Scoring is best ball under 8.1(b).

## 3.2 Bench

Thirteen bench places. The starting lineup and the bench together are the
**25-man Active Roster**.

## 3.3 Practice squad

(a) **Size.** A maximum of **seven** players.

(b) **Eligibility.** No more than **three** of the seven places may hold a player
who is not on a rookie contract; such a player reaches the practice squad only
on a practice squad contract under 5.16(c). Up to all seven places may hold a
player within his first two seasons since being drafted while on a rookie
contract.

(i) Eligibility runs from the player's **draft class**, not from the start date
of his current contract.

(ii) Trading a player does not restore expired eligibility. A trade ends one
contract and creates another; it does not change the player's draft class.

(c) **Cap and cash.** A practice squad player costs his team the full cash value
of his contract, exactly as an active player does. Against the cap, all bonuses
and all guaranteed money count in full wherever the player sits; **non-guaranteed
salary does not count against the cap while the player is on the practice
squad**.

(d) **Automatic return.** A player elevated from the practice squad to the
Active Roster returns to the practice squad automatically at **Tuesday 00:00**,
unless his owner holds him under (i). An owner who wants him on the Active Roster
for the coming week elevates him again before the compliance instant, or holds
him.

(i) **Holding a player up.** An owner may instead hold an elevated player on the
Active Roster. A held player does not return automatically; he stays on the
Active Roster until his owner moves him down, or until he is cut, traded or
locked under 3.3(i). A hold does not suspend the three-week limit: every week
the player is on the Active Roster at a compliance instant counts under 3.3(i),
and the fourth counted week locks him there for the season. A hold ends when the player leaves
the Active Roster by any path, and is not restored on its own.

(e) **The return always completes.** The automatic return is not an acquisition —
it restores a place the team already held — and is not limited by the practice
squad maximum. A practice squad left over its limit by the return is out of
compliance under 3.6 and is cured by the compliance instant.

(f) **Injured reserve.** A practice squad player is not eligible for injured
reserve. An injured player on the practice squad reaches injured reserve only by
being placed on the Active Roster first.

(g) **Poaching.** Any team may sign a player off another team's practice squad.
He is placed directly on the signing team's Active Roster and may never be signed
to its practice squad. Poaching is governed by 5.17.

(h) **Conversion to a practice squad contract.**

(i) The commissioner or co-commissioner may at any time move an active contract
to the practice squad where that contract otherwise satisfies the practice squad
requirements — one year, no void years, no option or restructure bonus, and
season cash not exceeding the league minimum. On such a movement the contract
becomes a practice squad contract and remains one. The conversion cannot be
reversed. Every eligibility test binds the officer exactly as it binds an owner:
the power is over *who may act*, not over *what the rules allow*.

(ii) The commissioner may additionally permit the same movement by any owner for
a stated period recorded on the League Calendar against 3.3(b).

(iii) A rookie contract is never converted. A rookie within his first two seasons
since being drafted sits on the practice squad on his rookie contract and needs
no conversion; a rookie past that window may not sit there at all.

(iv) Neither paragraph relaxes the limits in 3.3(a) or 3.3(b), and neither may
convert a player locked under 3.3(i)(iii).

(i) **The three-week limit.** A player on a practice squad contract, or a rookie
within two seasons of being drafted, who is on an Active Roster at **three**
weekly compliance instants in a single season has **one further return to the
practice squad** available to him. He is **locked onto the Active Roster** for
the remainder of that season on the earlier of:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(A) his next promotion from the practice squad to the
Active Roster; or

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(B) his presence on an Active Roster at a fourth weekly
compliance instant.

(i) A week counts if the player is on the Active Roster at that week's compliance
instant, whether or not he is moved down afterwards. Time on injured reserve
counts. A move between the practice squad and injured reserve, in either
direction, is not a promotion for the purposes of (A).

(ii) *Reserved.* This letter carried the 2026 Week 1 exclusion, now in Schedule
A.3.

(iii) **The lock.** A locked player may not be placed on a practice squad by any
authority, the commissioner's power under 3.3(h) included. On the day the lock
falls, a **practice squad contract becomes an ordinary active contract**; a
**rookie contract is not changed**, and the player continues to be paid exactly
what that contract provides. No salary, bonus or guarantee is altered by the
lock — see (j).

(iv) **Clearing waivers resets the count to zero and lifts the lock.** An owner
may cut the player, expose him to the wire, and — if he goes unclaimed — sign
him to a **new practice squad contract** and return him to the practice squad. A
rookie contract does not survive the clearance: the player returns on a one-year
practice squad contract at the practice squad maximum, or not at all.

(v) A player **claimed** off waivers keeps his count and his lock; he did not
clear, a team took him. **Except** that a player claimed back by the team that
waived him under 5.15(n) has his count reset to zero and his lock lifted,
exactly as if he had cleared. A player poached under 5.17 keeps his count: a
poach is a signing, not a clearance.

(j) **Promotion never costs a player money.** No player's salary, bonus or
guarantee for the current season may be reduced by his promotion to the Active
Roster, or by any change of contract type that the promotion causes.

## 3.4 Injured reserve

(a) A team may carry at most **ten** players on injured reserve.

(b) **Eligibility.** A player may occupy an injured reserve place only while he
carries an injury designation of **Injured Reserve, Out, Doubtful or Physically
Unable to Perform (PUP)**, as reported by the injury authority designated under
1.13(a)(iii). Those four designations, and no others, mark a player as injured
for every purpose in these rules.

(i) A placement on injured reserve of a player carrying no qualifying
designation is **flagged, not refused**. The placement stands; the roster is out
of compliance under 3.6(c); and the owner cures it by the weekly compliance
instant like any other overage.

(ii) Practice squad players — see 3.3(f).

(c) There is **no salary relief**, in cap or in cash, for a player on injured
reserve.

## 3.5 Position limits

(a) An Active Roster may hold at most **three quarterbacks**.

(b) An Active Roster may hold at most **three kickers**.

(c) Position for the purposes of this section is determined by the position
authority designated under 1.13(a)(ii).

## 3.6 Compliance

(a) **Off-Season.** Roster size is unlimited and the roster requirements are not
enforced. A roster not in compliance at the first instant of the In-Season is
brought into compliance by the commissioner before the first game of the regular
season, by cutting the most recently signed or drafted players first, with all
cap and cash consequences.

(b) **In-Season.** Compliance is measured at the weekly compliance instant under
1.4(f). A roster failing any test in (c) at that instant is out of compliance and
draws the fine in 6.7.

(i) The commissioner or co-commissioner brings a non-compliant roster into
compliance by **20:00** the same day, working with the owner where possible and
otherwise releasing players **last added, first released**. A release made this
way goes straight to free agency and does not pass through the waiver wire.

(ii) A player returning from injured reserve or from suspension gives his owner
**24 hours** to reach compliance, after which the fine is **\$50**. A returning
player is not eligible to score until the roster is compliant.

(c) **What compliance means.** A roster is compliant when every one of the
following holds at the same instant:

| Test | Limit | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Active Roster | no more than 25 | 3.1, 3.2 |
| Practice squad | no more than 7 | 3.3(a) |
| Practice squad non-rookies | no more than 3 | 3.3(b) |
| Injured reserve | no more than 10 | 3.4(a) |
| Injured reserve designations | every player there qualifies | 3.4(b) |
| Quarterbacks on the Active Roster | no more than 3 | 3.5(a) |
| Kickers on the Active Roster | no more than 3 | 3.5(b) |
| Salary Ceiling | not exceeded, once armed | 5.5(f) |

A roster of fewer than 25 players is out of compliance **only if it cannot field
the starting lineup in 3.1**. Short of 25 is otherwise compliant.

(d) *Reserved.*

(e) **Award-and-oblige limits.** A waiver award or a free agency award may take a
team past the Active Roster maximum to **28** players, or past the practice squad
maximum to **9**. It may not take a team past either of those ceilings, and it
may never take a team over its Salary Ceiling or below zero Owner Cash. A team
already past a ceiling is passed over and the next claim or offer wins. A team
taken past a maximum owes a corresponding move — a cut, a trade, or a purchase of
Owner Cash — to return to compliance by the next compliance instant.

# 4. ROOKIE DRAFT

## 4.1 Process

(a) The rookie draft is held annually, beginning **July 1st at 08:00**.

(b) Four rounds.

(c) **Repeating linear order** — 1 through 10, then 1 through 10 again. It is not
a snake draft.

(d) A selection is made within **24 hours** of the previous pick. Trading a pick
does not reset the clock.

(e) *Reserved.* This letter carried the requirement that the draft be conducted
on a named platform, which is not a rule of play. The letter is held open so that
citations to it resolve.

(f) The commissioner or co-commissioner may pause the draft at any time to
preserve league fairness, and should use that power sparingly.

## 4.2 Pick assignment

(a) The **10th** pick belongs to the first-place finisher.

(b) The **9th** pick belongs to the second-place finisher.

(c) The **8th** pick belongs to the third-place finisher.

(d) The **7th** pick belongs to the fourth-place finisher.

(e) Picks **1 through 6** are assigned by total points scored against during the
regular season, to discourage tanking, subject to the following:

(i) The team with the most points against receives the 1st pick, the team with the
second most the 2nd pick, and so on.

(ii) The 10th-place finisher is guaranteed no worse than the **3rd** pick.

(iii) The 9th-place finisher is guaranteed no worse than the **4th** pick.

(f) Rounds 2 through 4 repeat the round 1 order.

## 4.3 Eligibility

(a) Only rookies selected in the NFL entry draft for the current season are
eligible.

(b) The commissioner may grant eligibility to another player, including an
undrafted rookie free agent, on a request made by **June 30th**.

# 5. CONTRACTS

## 5.1 Contract dollars

Every reference in these rules to contract dollars, salaries, the salary cap,
Owner Cash, dead money or any other in-game money is for gameplay only and is
not real money. Section 2 and 2.1(c) are the exceptions.

## 5.2 Cap, Cash and PPV

The league uses three kinds of money. Each answers a different question and they
are not interchangeable.

(a) **Cap (Cap Charge).** The hard limit. Every contract carries a Cap Charge in
each season, and a team's total Cap Charge across its whole roster may not exceed
its Salary Ceiling for that season once 5.5(f) is armed.

(b) **Cash (Owner Cash).** What a team has actually spent. Signing bonuses come
out of cash in full at signing. Cash is tracked per season, may be earned,
penalised or purchased under 2.1(c), and is governed by 10.1.

(c) **PPV (Player Perceived Value).** A single number expressing what a contract
is worth to the player, used to compare contracts of different shapes. PPV is
never spent. It decides who wins a contested acquisition under 5.14, 5.17 and
6.1, and whether an extension satisfies 5.11(d).

(d) **PPV weighting.** A contract's PPV is the sum of its money, each kind
weighted by the season in which it falls:

| Money type | Yr 1 | Yr 2 | Yr 3 | Yr 4 | Yr 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Signing bonus | 100% | — | — | — | — |
| Guaranteed salary | 95% | 90% | 85% | 80% | 75% |
| Non-guaranteed salary | 30% | 20% | 15% | 10% | 5% |
| Roster bonus | 50% | 40% | 30% | 20% | 10% |
| Option bonus | — | 90% | 80% | 70% | 60% |

A signing bonus counts in full and entirely in Year 1.

## 5.3 Salary cap

(a) The league base salary cap for 2026 is **\$1,500**.

(b) The base cap moves each season by the same percentage as the real NFL salary
cap, rounded up under 1.9.

(c) The commissioner sets and publishes the base cap by **March 1st**, the first
day of the league year.

(d) A team's individual cap for a season is its **Salary Ceiling**, defined in
5.5.

(e) Every team must be under its Salary Ceiling by the first instant of the
In-Season.

## 5.4 Salary floors

(a) **Season Cap Floor.** Each team must spend at least **89%** of that season's
**base** cap. Spending is the team's total Cap Charge for the season, **dead
money included**, measured when the season's books close on **December 15**. The
floor is measured against the base cap every season, whatever rollover the team
carried into its own Salary Ceiling.

(b) **Season Cap Floor penalty.** A team below the floor has the shortfall — 89%
of the base cap less what it actually spent — deducted from its Owner Cash at the
December 15 close.

(c) **Rollover.** A team meeting the Season Cap Floor carries its unused cap
forward into the next season's Salary Ceiling as rollover, calculated under
5.5(c). A team failing the Season Cap Floor carries no rollover.

(d) **Multi-Season Cash Floor.** Over each Cash Window, a team must spend at
least **89%** of the sum of the league base caps for the seasons in that window,
measured in Owner Cash actually paid. This floor is separate from and additional
to (a): the Season Cap Floor is measured in Cap and tested every season; this
floor is measured in Cash and tested only at the end of a window.

(e) **Cash Windows.** The first Cash Window covers **2026, 2027 and 2028**. Each
window after it covers the next three seasons. A window is tested at the start of
the League Reset Period (00:01 on February 21st) following its final season.

(f) **What counts as Cash.** Cash spending has the meaning in 5.6(b): guaranteed
salary; non-guaranteed salary; a roster bonus in the season it converts; a
signing bonus in full in Year 1 only; and an option bonus in the season it
triggers. Cash purchased under 2.1(c) but not spent does not count. Owner Cash
deducted as a penalty does not count as spending.

(g) **Cash Floor penalty.** A team finishing a Cash Window below the floor
forfeits the shortfall from its Owner Cash at the start of the following League
Reset Period. Forfeited cash is not recoverable and does not count toward the
next window.

(h) **Rollover does not reduce the Cash Floor.** Carrying cap rollover forward
under 5.5 has no effect on a team's obligation under (d). A team may bank cap
room across as many seasons as it likes; the cash still has to be spent inside
the window.

## 5.5 Salary Ceiling

(a) A team's **Salary Ceiling** is its individual salary cap for a season:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*Salary Ceiling = league base cap + that team's rollover
from the immediately preceding season.*

(b) The base cap is set under 5.3(b) and (c).

(c) **Rollover** is determined automatically when the season's books close on
**December 15**. A team's rollover is its unused cap for the season then ending:
that team's Salary Ceiling for that season, less its total Cap Charge for that
season. Because unused cap is measured against the team's own Salary Ceiling and
not against the base cap, rollover carried in but not spent rolls forward again.
Unused cap is never less than zero. A team failing the Season Cap Floor in 5.4(a)
carries no rollover and is penalised under 5.4(b).

(d) **Worked example.** The 2026 base cap is \$1,500 and, with no preceding
season, Team A's 2026 Salary Ceiling is \$1,500. Team A commits \$1,400, leaving
\$100 unused at the December 15, 2026 close. The real NFL cap rises 10%, so the
2027 base cap is \$1,650. Team A's 2027 Salary Ceiling is \$1,750. Team A then
commits \$1,600 in 2027, leaving \$150 unused — measured against its own \$1,750
ceiling, not against the \$1,650 base. The real NFL cap rises 6%, so the 2028
base cap is \$1,749 and Team A's 2028 Salary Ceiling is \$1,899. Across the
2026–2028 window Team A must still meet the Multi-Season Cash Floor in 5.4(d),
which is 89% of \$1,500 + \$1,650 + \$1,749.

(e) **Rollover is carried one season at a time.** Each season's ceiling adds only
the immediately preceding season's unused cap. There is no separate accumulating
rollover balance and no multi-season rolling window.

(f) **The cap is a hard limit.** From the first instant of the In-Season, no
transaction may take a team over its Salary Ceiling — not a signing, not a trade,
not a waiver claim. A waiver claim that would breach the ceiling is passed to the
next team in order, and if no team can take the player he clears. There is no
percentage allowance, save (g). During the Off-Season and Dead Season the ceiling
is not enforced.

(g) **Auction exception.** A Blind Bid Auction win under 6.1 may push a team to
**125%** of its Salary Ceiling. That allowance applies year-round, including in
the Off-Season and Dead Season when (f) enforces nothing.

## 5.6 Minimum salary

(a) Every real (non-void) season of a contract must be worth at least the league
minimum for that season:

| 2026 | 2027 | 2028 | 2029 | 2030 | 2031 | 2032 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| \$9 | \$10 | \$10 | \$11 | \$11 | \$12 | \$13 |

The minimum rises 5% a season, rounded up under 1.9.

(b) **Cash only.** A season meets the minimum if the cash actually paid that
season reaches the figure above. Cash means: guaranteed salary; non-guaranteed
salary; a roster bonus in the season it converts; the signing bonus in full, in
Year 1 only; and an option bonus in the season it triggers. The prorated portion
of a signing or option bonus counts toward no season other than the one in which
the money is actually paid, and a season's Cap Charge has no bearing on whether
the minimum is met. A season may meet the minimum through salary, through bonus
money paid that season, or a combination. What this rule forbids is a season in
which less than the minimum actually changes hands.

(c) **Void seasons are exempt.** A void season carries no real salary by design.

(d) **Rookie and practice squad contracts are exempt.** A rookie contract's value
is set by the Rookie Wage Scale; a practice squad contract is capped from above
at the league minimum itself. Every other contract type — veteran free agent,
franchise tag, transition tag, fifth-year option — is subject to the minimum, as
is every auction bid.

(e) Salary is deemed paid one hour before the player's game time each week. A
player on bye is deemed paid one hour before the last game of the week.

## 5.7 Contract length

(a) The maximum contract length in real (non-void) seasons is **five**. A rookie
contract's length is fixed by 5.9(a).

(b) **Signing bonus proration.** A signing bonus is prorated evenly across the
contract's real seasons plus any **owner-elected void seasons**, to a total of
five. An owner may add void seasons to stretch a shorter contract to five. Void
seasons added automatically to hold an option bonus are **not** owner-elected and
never carry signing-bonus proration. Proration held in a void season is not
forgiven; it accelerates when the contract ends under 5.10(c).

(c) **Option bonus void seasons.** An option bonus automatically adds whatever
void seasons its five-season proration requires. They are created when the bonus
is **scheduled**, not when it triggers, and are not counted against the five-year
limit in (b). A contract's maximum possible span is therefore **nine** seasons:
five real plus four option-bonus void seasons.

## 5.8 Rookie contracts

Every rookie selected in the rookie draft signs a contract set entirely by the
Rookie Wage Scale. There is no negotiation. The value is adjusted for the
player's EDFL draft slot, not his real NFL draft position.

## 5.9 Rookie Wage Scale

(a) **Length.** A rookie contract covers only the seasons still ahead of the
player in his draft class window, not a fresh four seasons. A player of the 2023
class has one year; 2024, two; 2025, three; 2026, the full four.

(b) Pay escalates each year of the deal.

(c) Earlier picks carry a real signing bonus and heavier guarantees; later picks
are paid near the league minimum with little or no bonus.

| Draft position | How much is guaranteed |
|---|---|
| Round 1 | Fully guaranteed |
| Round 2 | Mostly guaranteed, declining through the round |
| Round 3 | A smaller minority guaranteed, declining through the round |
| Round 4, early picks | Only a small amount guaranteed |
| Round 4, late picks | League minimum, no guarantee |

(d) The full pick-by-pick values are maintained as a published league reference
table and are not negotiated at signing.

(e) **Fifth Year Option.** Only a Round 1 pick of a rookie draft is eligible. The
decision becomes available in the **third** season after the player's draft year,
and the option season is the **fourth**. Exercising the option adds one real,
fully guaranteed season to the player's existing rookie contract at the tier
price in (f). It **extends** that contract rather than creating a second one, so
the option year's guarantee becomes dead money if the player is later cut. The
owning team exercises or declines; the commissioner or co-commissioner may act
for a team and may reverse a decision within 96 hours. An exercised option is
exempt from the 30% Rule under 5.22(d).

(f) **Tiers and prices.** A player's tier is fixed by his EDFL performance across
the three seasons of his draft-class window, on the published EDFL Pro Bowl
record:

- **Tier 4** — two or more EDFL Pro Bowls
- **Tier 3** — one
- **Tier 2** — no Pro Bowl, but a startable finish in at least two of the three
  seasons (positional rank of QB 10th or better, RB 20th, WR 40th, TE 20th, K
  10th)
- **Tier 1** — none of the above

Prices are set per position and per option season as a fixed percentage of the
league base cap, so later seasons scale with the cap, and are published. For the
2027 option season, in 2026 dollars:

| Position | Tier 4 | Tier 3 | Tier 2 | Tier 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| QB | 168 | 125 | 62 | 52 |
| RB | 178 | 135 | 78 | 69 |
| WR | 218 | 162 | 96 | 86 |
| TE | 156 | 103 | 42 | 36 |
| K | 29 | 23 | 13 | 13 |

(g) **The EDFL Pro Bowl.** The EDFL Pro Bowl is a **published record, not a live
computation**. Forty-eight selections are made a season — 4 QB, 8 RB, 16 WR,
8 TE, 8 FLEX, 4 K — on a composite of season points and points per game over a
minimum of six games. The record is published once and never recomputed, so a
player's tier cannot change after his team has decided.

(h) The Rookie Wage Scale grows each year at the same rate as the real NFL salary
cap.

(i) *Moved.* This letter carried an explanation of why the guarantee profile in
(c) is shaped as it is. Explanation is not a rule; it is in the Technical Manual.
The letter is held open so that citations to it resolve.

## 5.10 Free agent contract calculation

(a) A free agent contract runs up to **five** seasons in total, of which up to
**two** may be owner-elected void seasons used only to spread a signing bonus.
Void seasons added automatically by an option bonus are separate and additional.

(b) A signing bonus is paid to the player immediately and, for cap purposes, is
spread evenly across every season of the deal, void seasons included. Void
seasons added automatically to hold an option bonus are excluded.

(c) **Proration accelerates when the contract ends.** A void season defers a
bonus's Cap Charge; it does not forgive it. When a contract reaches the end of
its last real season, every remaining prorated dollar — signing, option or
restructure bonus — is charged in full to the **following** season, the league
year the contract voids into. Seasons beyond that carry nothing.

## 5.11 Extensions

(a) **Windows.** An extension may be signed **March 1st through September 1st**,
and again during the Dead Season. No extension may be signed from September 2nd
through the EDFL Bowl.

(b) **Rookies.** A rookie may not be extended until his original rookie contract
has completed its third season, counted from his real NFL draft year.

(c) **Total length.** An extension may not push a player's total future
commitment to a team beyond **five** seasons combined. A player with two years
left may be extended by at most three new seasons.

(d) **Value.** A new extension must be worth at least as much, in PPV, as
everything remaining on the player's current contract.

(e) **The old contract.** Signing an extension closes out the old contract, which
is marked extended rather than deleted, and rolls any unpaid signing bonus into
the new contract's first season.

## 5.12 Retiring players

(a) The commissioner retires players.

(b) A player who publicly announces his retirement is retired, subject to the
commissioner's confirmation.

(c) **Players on no roster and not retired.** This clause does not apply to an
injured player.

(i) After Week 1, a player on no Active Roster and no practice squad who attended
no training camp may be treated as retired.

(ii) After Week 5, a player who has been on no Active Roster, signed by no team
and on no practice squad through the first five weeks may be treated as retired.

(iii) This clause never applies to an injured player expected to return, a player
voluntarily sitting out who expects to return, or a suspended player expected to
seek reinstatement.

(iv) The commissioner decides on any player an owner believes meets (i) or (ii)
**before** the owner takes any action on him.

(d) A player who retires before the season, or who does not play during the
season before being declared retired, has his team's cap and cash restored to the
position that stood before the beginning of the league year. Bonuses, option or
roster, and salary, guaranteed or non-guaranteed, are credited back.

(e) An owner is credited for prorated signing or option bonus money, cap and
cash, for every season **after** the retirement. Where the player retires during
the season and was rostered by an NFL team during that season, the owner recovers
nothing for that season.

## 5.13 Franchise and transition tags

Each team may place **one tag per league year** on a pending free agent, holding
him for one more season instead of letting him reach free agency.

(a) **Exclusive Franchise Tag.** A one-year contract at the greater of the average
Cap Charge of the top **five** EDFL contracts at the player's position, or 120%
of the player's prior-season Cap Charge. The player may not negotiate with any
other team.

(b) **Non-Exclusive Franchise Tag.** Priced the same way as the exclusive tag. The
player may negotiate with other teams. If he signs elsewhere, his original team
receives **two future first-round rookie draft picks** as compensation — unless
the original team exercises its right of first refusal and matches the offer.

(i) The signing team must hold two first-round picks. Where it holds more than
two, the furthest-out picks are the compensation. Where it holds several in the
same year, the receiving team chooses which.

(ii) Where more than one team makes an offer, the offer the original team must
match is the one with the highest total PPV made by a team able to provide two
first-round picks.

(iii) Where only one team bids and cannot provide two first-round picks, that
team has exclusive negotiating rights with the original team for **72 hours** to
agree compensation. The final decision rests with the original team.

(c) **Transition Tag.** A one-year contract at the greater of the average Cap
Charge of the top **ten** EDFL contracts at the position, or 120% of the player's
prior-season Cap Charge. The original team has the right of first refusal and may
match any competing offer, but receives no draft-pick compensation if it declines
to match.

(i) Where competing offers are equal, the offer of the team with the worse record
the previous season is the official competing offer. Where records are equal,
lower points for breaks the tie.

(d) Tags are not usable in the 2026 season.

## 5.14 In-Season free agency

In-season free agency is the league's path for acquiring a player who holds no
EDFL contract.

(a) **Who, and when.** Any player holding no active EDFL contract may be offered
one, except that a player released during the In-Season must first clear waivers
under 5.15. In-season free agency runs from the first instant of the In-Season to
the end of the regular season. It never pauses for a waiver run.

(b) **The clock.** The first valid offer on a player opens a **24-hour window** on
that player. Other owners may offer into it until it closes. A player has at most
one open window at a time.

(c) **Sealed.** While a window is open, no one — the commissioner and
co-commissioner included — sees any offer's terms or who made it. The only public
fact is whether the window is **contested**, meaning that more than one offer
exists. When the window resolves, every offer is published in full with the
offering team named.

(d) **The offer.** An offer is a complete contract proposal satisfying every rule
in this Section that governs the writing of a contract — the league minimum
(5.6), the Deion Rule (5.21), the 30% Rule (5.22) among them. It takes one of two
shapes: an **Active Roster contract** of any legal length, or a **practice squad
contract** under (f). No roster bonus and no option bonus may sit in the signing
season. An offer may be **revised upward** while the window is open. It may not
be withdrawn and may not be revised downward. An offer may be changed from a
practice squad contract to an active contract; it may not be changed the other
way.

(e) **The winner.** At close, the offer with the **highest total PPV** wins the
player at the exact terms offered. Where two offers tie on PPV, the **earlier**
offer wins, and a revision **keeps its original timestamp** — an owner who opens
a window does not lose his place by improving his own offer.

(f) **Practice squad offers.** A practice squad contract is one year and carries
no roster bonus, no option bonus and no void season. Its **total cash value may
not exceed the league minimum salary** for its season. Within that maximum the
owner chooses the shape. A practice squad contract is **exempt from the Deion
Rule**.

(g) **Mid-season money.** An offer is written as a full-season contract and tested
as written, but the salary actually charged for the signing season — cap and cash
alike — is prorated to the weeks the player is under contract, out of fourteen. A
Week 8 signing charges 7/14 of the written salary; a Week 14 signing charges
1/14. The signing bonus is charged in full. Seasons after the first are charged
as written.

(h) **Resolution and gates.** Nothing resolves unattended. The commissioner or
co-commissioner resolves a window after it closes, however late, and the signing
week is the week of resolution. **Owner Cash and the Salary Ceiling are hard
gates**: a winning offer the team cannot pay from that season's Owner Cash, or
that would take it past its Salary Ceiling, is passed over and the next-highest
offer wins at its own terms. The roster maximums are **award-and-oblige** under
3.6(e). The limit of three non-rookies on a practice squad in 3.3(b) is
award-and-oblige, not a gate.

(i) **Relationship to the auction.** The Blind Bid Auction in 6.1 is dormant, not
repealed. Should a tier be open, in-season free agency pauses: no new window
opens and a running window freezes until the tier is verified. The Player Value
Chart in 6.5 is not a reference for free agency.

(j) *Reserved.* This letter carried the 2026 start-up measures, now in Schedule
A.2.

(k) A player signed during a week earns salary for that week, whatever day he
signs.

## 5.15 Waivers

(a) Every player cut in season goes to the waiver wire.

(b) The wire **opens at Tuesday 00:00**, carrying every player cut since the
previous Tuesday 00:00, and is **adjudicated at Wednesday 00:00**. A player cut
after the wire opens waits for the following week's wire.

(c) **Priority** is season-to-date points for, lowest first, ties to most points
against. Priority is **positional, not consumable**: winning a claim does not move
a team down the order. The order is frozen at the moment the run starts, so a
later scoring correction cannot change who won.

(d) **An owner ranks his own claims.** The run walks the league order awarding
each team its highest-ranked unfulfilled claim, then repeats until a full pass
awards nothing. Each pass gives every team one attempt; a claim that is awarded
or passed over uses that team's turn for that pass.

(e) **Gates.** Owner Cash and the Salary Ceiling are hard. The roster maximums are
award-and-oblige under 3.6(e). A claim failing a gate is **passed over to the
next team in the order** — not to the same team's next claim. A player no team
can take **clears**.

(f) **Conditional cuts.** A claim may name one player on the claiming team's
roster to be cut if the claim wins. The named player is cut once however many
claims name him, and the team's remaining claims are re-tested against the roster
that results, the highest-ranked claim taking the freed room. A conditional cut
is itself an in-season cut and joins the **following** week's wire.

(g) While a player is on the wire his roster place is already open, and his team's
cap and cash show the worst case — the clear-waivers settlement. Nothing settles
until the run.

(h) A team is **not charged salary for a player it has waived**. The settlement is
computed through the week of the waive, not the week of the run.

(i) **A claimed player** is settled as a mid-season transfer: salary from the
waive instant forward is the claiming team's; bonus proration stays with and
accelerates onto the waiving team; no June 1st designation is consumed. The
claiming team pays for the full week in which it acquires him.

(j) **A cleared player** becomes a free agent immediately, and the waiving team's
dead money settles as an ordinary release.

(k) **A run is never reversed.** A cut may be reversed up to 24 hours after the
run only to resolve a matter the commissioner must rule on. It is not an option
an owner may request.

(l) **Playoffs.** The wire runs through the playoffs and is the only way to
acquire a player once the regular season ends. The last in-season wire is the
Tuesday of Week 17. Priority in the playoffs is fixed from the final
regular-season standings in the order **6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, 7, 8, 9, 10**.

(m) *Reserved.* This letter carried the 2026 wire start, now in Schedule A.4.

(n) **A team may claim back its own waived player.** A self-claim is placed,
ranked and settled like any other claim, and it resets the player's practice
squad count and lifts any lock under 3.3(i)(v).

(o) **Off-Season.** A player cut outside the In-Season is an immediate release and
does not pass through the wire. See Schedule B.

## 5.16 Practice squad contracts

(a) A practice squad player costs his team the full cash value of his contract,
the same as an active player. How that money counts against the cap is in 3.3(c).

(b) A drafted rookie may sit on the practice squad through his first two seasons
since being drafted with no change to his contract terms. Eligibility is measured
from his draft class under 3.3(b)(i).

(c) A non-rookie reaches the practice squad only on a **practice squad contract**
signed through in-season free agency under 5.14(f) — one year, at or under the
league minimum — or, during a period the commissioner has opened under 3.3(h)(ii),
by conversion of a qualifying contract.

(d) A practice squad contract expires under 5.24(a).

## 5.17 Poaching

Where this section is silent, 5.14 governs.

(a) **What poaching is.** Any team may sign a player off another team's practice
squad. A successful poach places the player **directly on the poaching team's
Active Roster** under a new contract. He may never be signed to the poaching
team's own practice squad. The original team receives no compensation.

(b) **The window.** A poach is initiated as an offer under 5.14 and opens a
**24-hour sealed window** on that player. Every rule of 5.14 applies except as
this section provides. The team holding the player's contract may bid in the
window; it may not open one on its own player. There is no first-offer exemption
on a poach. The window is resolved by the commissioner or co-commissioner after
it closes, however late, and the new contract's signing week is the week of
resolution.

(c) **The floor.** Every poach bid is at least a one-year Active Roster contract
carrying a signing bonus of at least **\$2** and total first-year cash of at
least the league minimum, and no winning bid may pay the player **less in the
current season than his current contract does**. There is no maximum. The floor
governs the first year only; every later year satisfies 5.6 on its own.

(d) **Prohibited terms.** A poach bid may carry no roster bonus and no option
bonus in any year, and no option void season. Ordinary void seasons are
permitted.

(e) **Who wins.** The highest total PPV wins. Where two or more bids tie, the team
holding the player's contract wins; failing that, the earliest submitted bid
wins.

(f) **Players on a rookie contract.** A bid on a player under a rookie contract —
the holding team's own bid included — wins only if its total PPV **exceeds** the
total PPV of that rookie contract, measured when the window opens. The holding
team need not bid: if no bid exceeds that value, the player and his rookie
contract are retained untouched and no transaction occurs. In that case, and only
in that case, the owner whose bid opened the window is fined **\$75** in Owner
Cash under 6.7.

(g) **Where the player lands.** Every winning bid places the player on the winning
team's Active Roster, including a winning bid by the team that already held him.
The sole exception is retention under (f), where nothing moves. A player retained
by his own team's winning bid is locked onto that team's Active Roster for the
season under 3.3(i). A player poached by another team keeps his 3.3(i) count.

(h) **The freeze.** From the moment a poach window opens until it is resolved, the
player's current contract may not be moved, cut, traded or restructured by
anyone, and the holding team may not elevate him.

(i) **Settlement.** On a successful poach, or on a retention by bid, the contract
being replaced settles for prorated signing and restructure bonus only: this
season's share stays where it is and future seasons' shares accelerate under
5.18, an in-season move falling under the automatic split in 5.18(c)(ii) so that
they land on the following season's cap. **Guaranteed salary not yet charged is
forgiven**; 5.18(b)(ii) does not apply. Weeks already charged stay with the
incumbent, and no cash is refunded or charged. The winning team pays for the full
week in which it acquires him.

(j) **Gates and compliance.** A poach award is gated and obliged exactly as a free
agency award under 5.14(h). A window in which no bid can be awarded is **void**;
nothing moves and no fine follows a void.

(k) **When poaching is allowed.** Poaching is open during the regular season only,
on the instants recorded on the League Calendar against this rule. Every poach
must be **initiated by 12:00 on the Saturday of Week 14**, so that no window
resolves after the final Sunday of the regular season. A window opened before the
cutoff takes bids until it closes.

(l) **Exemptions.** A team may designate up to **two** players on its practice
squad as **exempt from poaching** at any one time. No window may be opened on an
exempt player. An exemption stands until the owner releases it or the player
leaves the practice squad — by promotion, by a move to injured reserve, by a cut
or by a trade — and once it has ended it is not restored unless the owner
designates the player again. An exemption may not be placed on a player who
already has a poach window open on him. Whether a player is exempt is visible to
every owner.

(m) **Return from the Active Roster.** A player who has been on the Active
Roster may not be poached until he has been back on the practice squad for
**24 hours**. A player returned by the automatic return under 3.3(d) at Tuesday
00:00 is therefore poachable from Wednesday 00:00.

## 5.18 Dead money

(a) **Weekly salary accrual.** A season's guaranteed and non-guaranteed salary is
earned in **fourteen equal weekly shares**, one per regular-season game week.
Each share is charged, cash and cap, at **Tuesday 00:00**, in advance of the week
it covers. No salary is paid in the playoffs. Before the first salary instant of
a season, zero weeks have charged.

(b) **Releasing a player settles his contract immediately.**

(i) **Non-guaranteed salary.** Weeks already charged stay charged. Every uncharged
week of the current season and every future season is forgiven, cap and cash.

(ii) **Guaranteed salary.** Everything not yet charged — the remaining weeks of
the current season and all future seasons — accelerates immediately onto the
current season, in both cap and cash. Guaranteed salary can never be split,
deferred or pushed into a later season, a June 1st designation included.

(iii) **Signing bonus proration.** The current season's proration stays where it
is. All future seasons' prorations accelerate onto the current season's cap —
unless June 1st treatment applies under (c), in which case they are charged to
the following season instead. Signing bonus cash was paid at signing and is never
refunded.

(iv) **Option bonuses.** A **triggered** option bonus has its remaining prorations
treated exactly as signing bonus prorations above. An option bonus scheduled for
a season the player never reaches simply never happens — no cap charge, no cash,
no dead money.

(v) **Roster bonus.** Cut before September 2nd: that season's roster bonus never
converts and is forgiven entirely, cap and cash. Cut on or after September 2nd:
it converted and stays charged. Future seasons' roster bonuses are forgiven
either way.

(c) **June 1st treatment.** Two distinct rules.

(i) **Elected designation.** A cut made between **March 1st and May 31st** may be
designated a June 1st cut, charging future-season bonus prorations to the
following season's cap instead of accelerating them. **Each team may elect at
most two June 1st designations per league year.** Only elections count against
the limit.

(ii) **Automatic split.** Any cut made from **June 1st through February 20th**
receives the same split automatically, and consumes no designation.

(iii) In both cases, unpaid guaranteed salary still accelerates fully onto the
current season under (b)(ii) and cannot ride the split.

(d) **When cuts are allowed.** An owner may cut any player on his own roster at
any time, without limit, except during the **League Reset Period**, when rosters
are frozen. A cut does not always free cap space: cutting forgives unearned
non-guaranteed salary and future roster bonuses, but it also accelerates all
remaining guaranteed salary and, outside June 1st treatment, all future bonus
prorations onto the current season. A heavily front-loaded contract can cost more
cut than kept.

(e) **Waived players.** A player waived and claimed passes his contract to the
claiming team under 5.15(i): all prorated money accelerates to the original team
under (b)(iii) and (b)(iv), and all future salary and bonuses become the claiming
team's. A player waived and unclaimed is treated as released under this section.

(f) A player on the waiver wire is charged to no one. The week attaches to
whoever holds him after the run, or to nobody if he clears.

## 5.19 Contract restructuring

A restructure converts salary a team still owes this season into a signing bonus
paid immediately, spreading its Cap Charge across later seasons.

(a) **Windows.** A restructure is allowed from **March 1st through the trade
deadline**, and again during the Dead Season. Contracts are frozen during the
League Reset Period.

(b) **Who may restructure.** Any owner may restructure a contract on his own
roster. The commissioner and co-commissioner may act for any team.

(c) **What converts.** Only money not yet paid. Guaranteed and non-guaranteed
salary for the current season are both eligible, reduced by the share already
earned at 1/14 a week under 5.18(a). A roster bonus that has already converted is
not eligible, nor is the original signing bonus, nor an option bonus already
triggered.

(d) **A new bonus, not a bigger one.** The converted amount becomes a **new**
signing bonus with its own proration window of **two to five seasons**, beginning
with the current season. It does not join the original signing bonus and does not
re-spread it.

(e) **Real years are fixed.** The number of real seasons does not change. Void
seasons are added automatically where a proration window runs past the end of the
contract.

(f) **The player may not be worse off.** A restructure must **strictly increase**
the contract's PPV.

(g) **Rules that still apply.** The Deion Rule applies in every real season,
including a season receiving only proration. The minimum salary applies to the
current season. The Salary Ceiling applies once armed. The 30% Rule does not
apply — see 5.22(f).

(h) **Rookie contracts.** A rookie contract may not be restructured until after
the completion of the player's third season, counted from his draft class.

(i) **Cooling-off period.** From **March 1, 2027**, a contract may not be
restructured in the same league year it was signed.

(j) **Whole dollars.** A converted amount is a whole number of dollars. Proration
divides evenly across the window, the final proration season carrying any
remainder.

(k) **Reversal.** The commissioner or co-commissioner may reverse a restructure
within **96 hours**.

## 5.20 Option bonuses

An option bonus locks in an additional year of a veteran free agent's contract in
advance.

(a) **Eligibility.** Only on a veteran free agent contract, and only for contract
**Year 2 or later**.

(b) **Scheduling.** A team schedules an option bonus at signing, or later through
a restructure, attaching an amount to a specific future contract year. No
separate act of exercise is needed when that year arrives. Scheduling the bonus
immediately extends the contract with the void seasons its proration will require
under 5.7(c).

(c) **Automatic trigger.** The bonus becomes a real, binding part of the contract
on **March 1st** of its scheduled season. If the player is cut before that date
the bonus simply never happens.

(d) **Proration.** Once triggered, the bonus prorates like a signing bonus, spread
evenly across exactly **five** seasons beginning with its own exercise year.

(e) **Dead money.** If the player is cut after the bonus has triggered, proration
not yet counted comes due as dead money exactly as an ordinary signing bonus
would. Proration remaining when the contract reaches its natural end is not
forgiven either; it accelerates under 5.10(c).

## 5.21 The Deion Rule

In any real, non-void season of a contract, that season's actual salary —
guaranteed salary, non-guaranteed salary, roster bonus, and any option bonus paid
that year, added together — must be **at least as much as that season's prorated
signing bonus charge**. Void seasons are exempt. A practice squad contract is
exempt under 5.14(f).

## 5.22 The 30% Rule

(a) **Compensation defined.** A season's compensation is its guaranteed salary,
non-guaranteed salary, roster bonus, and the prorated share of any option bonus
charged to that season, added together. **Signing bonus money — both the payment
and its proration — is excluded entirely.**

(b) **The limit.** In every season after a contract's first, compensation may
exceed the immediately preceding season's compensation by no more than **30% of
the contract's Year 1 compensation**. The allowed step is fixed by Year 1 and does
not compound. Decreases are always allowed.

(c) **Applies to bids and offers.** An auction bid and an in-season free agency
offer are complete contract proposals and must satisfy this rule to be submitted.

(d) **Exemptions.** Rookie contracts and Fifth Year Options are exempt.

(e) **Grandfather.** The contracts identified under Schedule A.5 are permanently
exempt. A grandfathered player who signs an extension is not carried over: an
extension creates a new contract, and a new contract is never exempt.

(f) **Restructures are exempt.** A restructure does not re-test this rule.

## 5.23 Cutting a player

(a) An owner cutting a player in season elects one of two timings.

(i) **Immediate.** The roster place opens at once and the player goes to the
wire. Cash and cap are pending until the run.

(ii) **End of the week.** The player keeps his roster place and keeps scoring.
The cut takes effect at the end of the week's last game, which is before the next
salary instant, so the player never costs his team the following week.

(b) A designation may be changed to an immediate cut, or withdrawn entirely, at
any time until it fires. It is visible to every owner. A player traded before his
designation fires **loses the designation**; it does not travel with him.

(c) A designated player still occupies a roster place. A designation cannot be
used to reach compliance.

(d) A cut made after the player's NFL game has begun becomes an end-of-week
designation.

(e) Settlement on a cut is governed by 5.18 and the wire by 5.15.

## 5.24 Contract expiry

(a) **Practice squad contracts and playoff contracts expire on the Tuesday after
Week 17.** Those players become unrestricted free agents and are not eligible for
restricted free agent status, the franchise tag or the transition tag. A rookie
contract does **not** expire then, even if the rookie finished the season on the
practice squad.

(b) **Active contracts run to the start of the League Reset Period, February
21st.**

# 6. AUCTIONS, FINES AND THE LEAGUE FUND

## 6.1 Blind Bid Auction

An asynchronous sealed auction, designed to work across time zones. It is
**dormant, not repealed**. Nothing in this section operates unless the
commissioner opens a tier.

(a) **Format.** Free agents are grouped into tiers, one tier open at a time. A
tier stays open for a multi-day bidding window rather than a live countdown, with
dates set by the commissioner for each auction.

(b) **Sealed bids.** While a tier is open, every bid is sealed from everyone, the
commissioner included. No one in any role sees a bid's contents or who placed it
until the tier's window closes. The only public information during the window is
a rough interest level per player — how many bids exist — with no amounts and no
identities.

(c) **A bid is a complete contract proposal**, built under Section 5, not a single
number. The **highest total PPV** bid on a player wins him at the exact terms
proposed.

(d) **Revisions.** An owner may revise a bid at any point before the tier closes.
The last bid submitted is the official bid. A revision replaces the prior bid
entirely, including its position for tie-breaking under (e).

(e) **Ties.** Where two or more bids on a player tie on PPV, the tie goes to
whichever bid, counting the timing of revisions, was placed earliest.

(f) **Resolution.** After a tier closes, results settle in stages.

(i) **Evaluation.** The commissioner evaluates the tier; the winning bid for each
player is the highest total PPV. No contracts are created at this stage.

(ii) **Flags.** Each winning team's combined wins in the tier are checked, in
aggregate, against two limits: the team may not be pushed past **125%** of its
Salary Ceiling under 5.5(g), and its total incoming cash obligations may not
exceed its available Owner Cash. A team over either limit is flagged.

(iii) **The 24-hour window.** A flagged owner has 24 hours from evaluation to
become compliant — by clearing cap space, by purchasing Owner Cash under 2.1(c),
by reshaping a winning contract at the same PPV, or by agreeing with the
commissioner to surrender one or more wins.

(iv) **Passing a win over.** If a flag remains after the 24-hour window, the
commissioner surrenders wins on the owner's behalf, most recently submitted
winning bid first, then the next most recent, until the owner is compliant. A
player whose winning bid is passed over is awarded to the next-highest bid on
him, which is itself then checked under (ii); if no other bid exists, he goes
unsigned in that tier.

(g) **Verification and publication.** Once no flags remain, the commissioner
verifies the tier. At that moment, and not before, every winning bid becomes a
real contract and the tier's results become public: every bid in full with the
bidding team named — winning, losing and passed over alike — together with any
option bonuses offered. Verified results are final under 1.11(c).

(h) **Transparency after verification.** Publication under (g) is complete. A
losing bid is not a private matter once its tier is settled. Nothing in this
clause disturbs the seal in (b), which operates only until verification. A
**withdrawn** bid is not a result and is never published; it remains visible only
to the team that made it and to the commissioner and co-commissioner.

## 6.4 Delegated bidding

(a) An owner may pre-author sealed bids for an open auction tier. A delegated bid
is validated against the same rules as any other bid and becomes a real sealed
bid the moment the owner approves it. It is indistinguishable from a manual bid
in resolution, in tie-breaking under 6.1(e), and in results.

(b) **Interest levels.** An owner may tag a player **Must-have (1.25×), Target
(1.05×), Depth (0.90×)** or **Flyer (0.75×)**, which scales the suggested bid
from the Player Value Chart. The multipliers are fixed by the league and are not
owner-adjustable. An owner's tags, targets and exposure limits are sealed to the
same standard as bids under 6.1(b) — not visible to any other owner, the
commissioner included, at any time, before or after the tier closes.

(c) **Exposure limits.** An owner may cap the number of delegated bids and the
worst-case cash and cap exposure of his slate. A bid exceeding a limit is
**skipped** in the owner's stated priority order rather than reduced. The number
of wins cannot be capped in a sealed simultaneous auction, and an exposure limit
is not a defence to the flag process in 6.1(f).

(d) **Manual bids take precedence.** A manual bid on a player stands down any
delegated entry for that player, and a player already bid on manually cannot be
delegated. A delegated bid, once submitted, is revised like any other bid, and
revising or re-approving resets its tie-break position under 6.1(e).

(e) **Withdrawal.** A delegated bid may be withdrawn on the same terms as any
other bid under 6.6. Withdrawing it also cancels the delegated entry that
produced it.

## 6.5 Player Value Chart

(a) The commissioner publishes and maintains the Player Value Chart, a league-wide
reference of player values stated in total PPV at a likely contract length. It is
distributed to every owner, and every previously published version remains
available.

(b) **Chart values are reference information only.** No owner is required to bid
any particular amount, and a player's absence from the chart does not prevent
bidding on him.

(c) The chart is **not** a reference for in-season free agency under 5.14(i).

## 6.6 Bid withdrawal

(a) A team may withdraw a submitted bid at any time before its tier closes. A
withdrawn bid is void: it cannot win, it is not counted in the public interest
total, and it takes no further part in the auction.

(b) Each team may withdraw a limited number of bids per tier, equal to the number
of players in that tier divided by five, rounded up, with a minimum of **one** and
a maximum of **five**.

(c) A team that has withdrawn a bid on a player may bid on that player again in
the same tier. Doing so does not restore the withdrawal; every withdrawal counts
against the limit.

(d) Withdrawing a delegated bid also cancels the delegated entry that produced it.

(e) **No bid may be withdrawn once its tier has closed.** A winning bid a team
cannot afford is handled under 6.1(f) by pass-over, not by withdrawal.

(f) No record of a withdrawal is published, before or after verification. A
withdrawal is visible only to the team that made it and to the commissioner and
co-commissioner.

(g) Only a submitted bid may be withdrawn. A delegated entry that was skipped,
failed or was never armed produced no bid; removing it is a cancellation, costs
nothing, and does not count against (b). The limit in (b) is a ceiling on
withdrawals, not an entitlement.

## 6.7 Compliance fines and the League Fund

(a) A roster out of compliance at the weekly compliance instant draws a fine of
**\$150 per violation** in Owner Cash. Roster size and cap are separate
violations, and so is a player holding an injured reserve place without a
qualifying designation.

(b) An owner who brings his **own** roster into compliance before **20:00** the
same day pays **\$50** instead.

(c) From a team's **fourth** violation in a season the fine rises by **\$50** each
time — \$200, \$250, and so on.

(d) Fines are imposed on the **following Tuesday at 16:00** and may take Owner
Cash below zero. **A team with negative Owner Cash may make no transaction that
costs money.**

(e) The fine under 5.17(f) — opening a poach window on a rookie contract and
failing to beat it — is **\$75** and is imposed and paid the same way.

(f) Fines are paid into the **League Fund** and distributed at the end of the
season.

# 7. TRADES

## 7.1 Tradeable assets and settlement

(a) Any player on a current EDFL Active Roster, practice squad or injured reserve
may be traded, alone or packaged. Multi-team trades are permitted.

(b) Future rookie draft picks up to **three years** out may be traded. A new
year's picks become tradeable immediately after the final selection of the
current draft.

(c) The receiving team takes over the existing contract and the remaining salary
obligations on it.

(d) Prorated bonus money remaining in a contract **accelerates forward to the
current year** when a player is traded. The giving team is responsible for it and
it cannot be part of the trade.

(e) **June 1st treatment for trades.** A trade is never eligible for an elected
June 1st designation and never consumes one.

(i) A trade effective between **March 1st and May 31st** accelerates all remaining
bonus proration into the current league year. There is no election available.

(ii) A trade effective from **June 1st** onward splits future-season bonus
prorations to the following league year automatically, and consumes no
designation.

(iii) *Moved.* This numeral carried the explanation of why (e) differs from a
cut's June 1st treatment. It is in the Technical Manual. The numeral is held open
so that citations to it resolve.

(iv) Unpaid guaranteed salary is not dead money in a trade; it transfers with the
player under (f).

(f) **What transfers and what stays.** Only bonus proration stays with the giving
team. A trade transfers all guaranteed and non-guaranteed salary from the
effective date forward to the receiving team, in both cap and cash.

(i) Signing bonus proration, and the proration of any option bonus that has
already triggered, stay with the giving team and accelerate under (e).

(ii) An option bonus that has **not** yet triggered travels with the contract to
the receiving team at its original exercise year and amount.

(iii) A roster bonus that has already converted stays charged to the giving team.
An unconverted roster bonus travels.

(g) **In-season trades.** Weeks already charged stay with the giving team,
guaranteed and non-guaranteed alike. The receiving team assumes the remaining
weeks only.

(h) **When the settlement is fixed.** A trade's cap and cash consequences are
determined at the moment the **last involved owner accepts** — not when an officer
approves it, and not when it is executed. Every date-sensitive test in the
settlement, including the June 1st boundary in (e), the roster bonus conversion
in (f)(iii) and the weekly accrual count in (g), is taken at that instant.

(i) **A transferred contract is not re-underwritten.** The Deion Rule, the league
minimum and the 30% Rule govern the writing of a contract, not its transfer. A
trade changes who owes the terms, not the terms. A grandfathered exemption under
5.22(e) survives a trade.

## 7.2 Restrictions

(a) 7.1 is the definitive statement of what may be traded. The following list
exists to remove any doubt. None of these may be traded or promised in a trade:

(i) Real money or any asset outside the game.

(ii) Favors, including help of any kind, and voting rights on rules or on any
other matter.

(iii) Anything executing at a later date so as to circumvent a rule, including a
promise of an asset further out than three years.

(iv) Owner Cash, cap space and dead money.

## 7.3 Conditional compensation

(a) A condition may be attached only to a **future draft pick**. A player to be
named later is not permitted.

(b) A pick may be conditioned on highest-of, lowest-of, or protected status.

(c) A pick may be conditioned on a performance metric. Where a pick is part of
pending compensation, the team must maintain the required asset until the metric
is adjudicated. Where a **specific** pick is named, it may not be traded to
anyone else. Where a **general** pick is named — a year and a round — the team
must maintain at least one pick in that year and round, not necessarily the same
one.

(d) A pick tied to compensation may carry **one** condition only.

## 7.4 Trade-backs and windows

(a) **Direct trade-backs are not allowed in the same league year.** Where Team A
trades Player X to Team B, Team B may not trade Player X back to Team A from
March 1st until the conclusion of the EDFL Bowl. Where Team B trades Player X to
Team C and Team C later trades him to Team A, that is permitted provided:

(i) it is not part of a multi-team trade including Team A and Team B; and

(ii) the first and second trades do not fall in the same trade window.

(b) **There are three trade windows:**

(i) March 1st to September 1st;

(ii) September 2nd to the trade deadline;

(iii) the end of the EDFL Bowl to February 21st, the Dead Season.

(iv) **No trade at all** is permitted from the trade deadline until the end of the
EDFL Bowl, or during the League Reset Period.

(c) **Temporary suspension.** The commissioner may suspend (a), in whole or in
part, for a stated period recorded on the League Calendar against this rule. A
trade is judged against the rule as it stood at the moment the last party
accepted it, in keeping with 7.1(h). A suspension does not relax the windows in
(b), the deadline in 7.5, or any cap, cash or roster requirement.

## 7.5 Trade deadline

(a) The trade deadline is **23:59 on the Monday concluding Week 12** of the
regular season — one minute before the Tuesday 00:00 instant at which Week 13
salary is charged and the wire opens under 1.4(f).

(i) Legality is judged on the timestamp of the trade's acceptance under 7.1(h).

## 7.6 Notification

(a) A trade is notified by one owner sending its details to all owners, and every
other owner involved accepting. Notification is not complete until every involved
owner has accepted.

(b) *Reserved.* This letter carried the requirement that a trade be entered on
the league's play platform, which is an administrative step rather than a rule of
play. The letter is held open so that citations to it resolve.

(c) **A proposal is private to its parties.** A proposed trade is visible only to
the owners party to it until every party has accepted. From that moment it is
public and awaits processing under 7.7. A draft that has not been sent is visible
only to the owner building it. **Neither the commissioner nor the co-commissioner
may see a proposal he is not party to.** A declined, cancelled or expired proposal
stays private to its parties permanently.

(d) **Overlapping offers.** An owner may name the same player or pick in any
number of open proposals, to the same owner or to different owners. **Offering a
player does not reserve him.** Only a trade every party has accepted reserves an
asset: on the last acceptance, that trade takes the player or pick, and every
other open proposal naming the same asset is cancelled automatically, with the
reason recorded and shown to the owners affected. Where two offers are accepted
at the same moment, the first to complete takes the asset and the second is
refused. A draft is not an offer and is not cancelled; it simply cannot be sent
while the asset it names is committed elsewhere.

## 7.7 Approval, veto and reversal

(a) **Processing time.** A trade takes 24 hours to process and approve after
notification.

(b) During the rookie draft only, trades are processed as soon as possible to
allow pick trading. There is no guarantee a trade will be approved in time.

(c) **Approval.** The commissioner and co-commissioner have equal authority to
approve a trade. An approver under 1.2(d) may act only where both have recused
themselves or are unavailable within the 24-hour window.

(d) **Competitive balance.** Owners are independent and may make whatever trades
they wish. Where a trade is so lopsided as to be grossly unfair to other owners,
or indicates collusion, **the commissioner and the commissioner alone** may
disapprove it. A trade involving the commissioner's own team may be disapproved
for competitive balance only by a grievance vote. A disapproval may be appealed
through the grievance process.

(e) **Conflict of interest.** Where a conflict of interest exists, the
commissioner and co-commissioner must recuse themselves from approval. Any trade
involving a player or asset of an officer's own team is automatically a conflict
of interest for that officer.

(f) **Players mid-trade.** A player moving between teams may not be used on the
active roster of either team during processing.

(g) *Reserved.* This letter carried the interaction between the 24-hour
processing period and the league's play platform, which is not a rule of play. The
letter is held open so that citations to it resolve.

(h) **Reversal of an executed trade.** After a trade has been executed the
commissioner may reverse it. A reversal restores the position as it stood
immediately before execution: each player returns to the team that sent him on
his original contract, each draft pick returns to the team that held it, and
neither team's cap nor Owner Cash retains any charge, credit or dead money
arising from the trade. **A reversed trade is not erased**; it remains on the
public record marked reversed, with the reason given, and is logged under 1.11.

(i) **Authority and recusal.** The commissioner may reverse any executed trade,
including one involving his own team. The co-commissioner may reverse any
executed trade **except** one involving his own team. This departs deliberately
from (e).

(ii) **Window.** A reversal must be made within **96 hours of execution**. The
window runs from execution, not from the moment the settlement was fixed under
7.1(h).

(iii) **Conditions.** A reversal is available only while **all** of the following
hold. Each is absolute and none may be waived:

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(A) the trade was executed in the current season;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(B) every player received is still held on the contract the
trade created, still active, with nothing recorded against it;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(C) no auction tier has been verified since the trade was
executed;

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(D) every draft pick that moved is still held by the team
that received it and has not been used; and

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(E) the window in (ii) remains open.

(iv) **Reason.** A reversal requires a stated reason, published in the Action Log
and shown on the trade itself.

(v) **Compliance after reversal.** Where the cap is enforced under 5.5(f) or the
roster under 3.6, a reversal that would leave either team in breach of its Salary
Ceiling, its Owner Cash floor or the Active Roster limit is **refused**. The
commissioner may nevertheless proceed, in which case the reversal is recorded as
**forced**, the breach is named in the Action Log, and the team affected must cure
it under 3.6.

(vi) **Beyond the window or the conditions.** A trade failing any condition in
(iii), or falling outside the window in (ii), is not reversible. Correcting it is
a deliberate commissioner action under 1.11(b), taken on the record.

(vii) *Moved.* This numeral carried the effect of a reversal on the league's play
platform, which is an administrative consequence rather than a rule. It is in the
Technical Manual. The numeral is held open so that citations to it resolve.

## 7.9 Trade block, watchlist and the rumour desk

Nothing in this section moves a player, changes a contract or costs a team money.
It governs what the league is told, and by whom.

(a) **The trade block.** An owner may place any player he holds under an active
contract on the league trade block — Active Roster, practice squad or injured
reserve alike. The block is **advisory and binds no one**. It is scoped to the
contract: it ends when the contract ends, however it ends, and ends the moment the
player is waived. A restructure neither ends it nor resets it.

(i) A block lasts **14 days**. The owner may remove it at any time and his removal
is absolute. Where any watchlist interest in the player exists at the 14-day mark,
at any visibility tier, the block **stays up**, and falls off the moment the last
interest is removed. An expired block is not revived by new interest; only the
owner re-marking the player restores it, and re-marking resets the 14 days.

(ii) A **shop** submission under (c) about a player the submitting owner holds
places the block on that contract on the same terms, and resets its clock if one
is standing.

(b) **The watchlist.** An owner may mark interest in any player, at one of three
visibility tiers chosen per player: **private**, **shared with the player's
owner**, or **public to the league**. Interest is scoped to the player, not the
contract, and survives his being traded, cut or waived; it persists until the
owner removes it.

(i) A tier may be changed at any time in either direction, but visibility runs
forward only: **what was public cannot be made retroactively unseen**. When the
player changes teams, a shared-with-owner marker reverts to private.

(ii) **Private interest is unattributed, not hidden.** The league reads private
markers only in aggregate, without names, and that aggregate is neither bucketed
nor lagged. An owner who infers who holds a marker has broken no rule. The private
tier has no commissioner read.

(iii) A watchlist marker never becomes a rumour desk submission, automatically or
by offer.

(c) **The rumour desk.** An owner may submit to the league's rumour desk one claim
at a time: a **subject** — one player, one draft pick or one rookie prospect; a
**direction** — acquire, shop, sign a free agent, release or draft; optionally
what he would give and what he seeks; a **tier**; and a **delay** — now, tonight
or this week. The desk publishes it after the delay.

(i) **The three tiers.** A **leak** is published as a rumour with no source named
or implied. **Off the record** is published as confirmed, with the desk declining
to name its source. **On the record** is published as confirmed and attributed to
the submitting team by name. The league additionally sees an aggregated view, one
row per subject and direction, rated **Maybe, Likely or Confirmed** by tier, with
a count of sources.

(ii) An owner may submit information about **another owner's** intentions at the
**leak tier only**. Off the record and on the record are reserved for the
submitter's own players, picks and intentions. The desk never names a third party
as a source.

(iii) **A submission need not be true.** An owner may file a leak he knows to be
false. No owner who acts on a published rumour has a grievance under 1.3 for
having believed it. What the desk publishes as confirmed is confirmed only in the
sense that a named or unnamed owner said it.

(iv) A submission stays live for **14 days** and may be withdrawn before it
publishes. Two live submissions naming the same subject in the same direction
corroborate one another and the desk's phrasing escalates with the count;
corroboration never changes a rating.

(v) *Moved.* This numeral carried how the desk composes its prose, which is a
matter for the Technical Manual, not a rule of play. It is held open so that
citations to it resolve.

(d) **The privacy boundary.** The rumour desk **may read the trade block freely.
It may never read the watchlist**, at any tier, in aggregate or otherwise. A false
leak has no record behind it; a leaked watchlist entry does, and the owner it
names never agreed to be named.

(e) **Rookie prospects.** The league keeps a board of the coming rookie class —
the grades and ranks of the prospect source designated under 1.13(a)(iv), as
published, filtered to the positions the league plays, nothing derived — so that a
draft rumour has a subject. The board rolls to the next class when the
commissioner closes the league's rookie draft.

# 8. GAMEPLAY

## 8.1 Format

(a) Games are head-to-head, the winner being the team scoring the most points
that week.

(b) **Best Ball.** Each team fields a nominal starting lineup of 1 QB, 2 RB, 4 WR,
2 TE, 2 FLEX and 1 K, plus a 13-man bench, together the 25-man Active Roster.
**No lineup is set or changed.** Each week, the highest-scoring eligible player at
each position across the full Active Roster counts toward the team's score, bench
included. This does not extend to the practice squad: **a practice squad player
is never eligible to score.** A player on injured reserve is never eligible to
score.

(c) **A week counts in the standings only once it is final.** A week is final four
hours after its last kickoff, with the week's scores taken in since that instant.

## 8.2 Schedule

# 9. STANDINGS AND PLAYOFFS

## 9.1 Regular season

(a) The league is divided into two divisions of five, the **Champions League** and
the **Developmental League**.

(i) **Realignment** occurs in the off-season. The top five teams of the previous
season are placed in the Champions League and the bottom five in the Developmental
League.

(b) The regular season is the first **fourteen** weeks of the NFL regular season.

(c) **Standings tie-breakers**, division winners determined first:

(i) head-to-head record;

(ii) total points for;

(iii) total points against;

(iv) coin toss, heads assigned to the team whose three-letter code comes first
alphabetically.

## 9.2 Playoffs

(a) **Six teams** make the playoffs:

| Seed | How it is earned |
|---|---|
| 1 | Best record — first-round bye |
| 2 | The other division winner |
| 3 | Better record of the two second-place division teams |
| 4 | Worse record of the two second-place division teams |
| 5 | Best record of the remaining teams, on the tie-breakers in 9.1(c) |
| 6 | Best **points for** among the remaining teams |

(i) Seed 6 is decided on points for and not on record. A 6–8 team with more points
for than a 7–7 team that lost the tie-breaker for the 5th seed takes the 6th seed.

(b) **Wild card round**, NFL Week 15: 3 v 6 and 4 v 5. The winners advance; the
losers play the fifth-place game in Week 16.

(c) **Semifinals**, NFL Week 16: 1 v the lowest remaining seed, 2 v the second
lowest. The winners go to the EDFL Bowl; the losers play the consolation game.

(d) **The EDFL Bowl and the consolation game** are played in NFL Week 17. The
fifth-place game is played in Week 16, alongside the semifinals.

(e) **Prizes.**

(i) 1st — the winner of the EDFL Bowl receives the first-place prize, including the
Player's Cup.

(f) 2nd — the loser of the EDFL Bowl receives the second-place prize.

(g) 3rd — the winner of the consolation game receives the third-place prize.

(h) 4th — the loser of the consolation game receives the fourth-place prize.

(i) 5th — the winner of the fifth-place game receives the fifth-place prize.

(j) 6th — the loser of the fifth-place game receives the sixth-place prize.

(k) **Playoff tie-breakers.** Where a playoff game is tied, it is decided in this
order:

(i) QB score.

(ii) WR score, not including flex.

(iii) RB score, not including flex.

(iv) TE score.

(v) Flex score.

(vi) K score.

(vii) Coin toss.

(l) **Weeks 15 to 17 carry no salary and no contracts.** Cutting in the playoffs
costs nothing for the current season; a player with future guaranteed years still
carries the usual acceleration.

(m) A team that misses the playoffs may cut players after Week 14.

## 9.3 Toilet Bowl

(a) The last four teams in the standings play a loser-takes-all tournament
alongside the playoffs.

(i) Teams are seeded in reverse order of standings.

(ii) **The losing team advances.**

(iii) The "winner" of the Toilet Bowl is the team that loses the tournament.

(iv) A 7th-place game is played to set the final order for the draft lottery.

# 10. LEAGUE REVENUE

## 10.1 Owner Cash accounts

(a) Each team has an Owner Cash account for each season. Available cash is always
computed, never set by hand: the season's starting amount, plus adjustments, less
cash actually spent on contracts.

(b) An adjustment outside normal contract spending — a purchase under 2.1(c), a
penalty under 5.4, or a correction — is recorded as an individual transaction in
a ledger with a note explaining it, and is recorded in the Action Log under 1.11.

(c) Every owner may audit his own account at any time: the starting amount, every
transaction with its note, cash spent, and the resulting available balance.

## 10.2 Annual revenue share

Each off-season, owners vote on the revenue share each team receives for the
coming season. It is not an equal amount league-wide. See Schedule B.

# 11. DEFINITIONS

**30% Rule** — The limit on year-over-year rises inside one contract: no season's
compensation may exceed the prior season's by more than 30% of Year 1
compensation, signing bonus excluded. See 5.22.

**Active Roster** — The 25 players making up a team's starting lineup and bench
together, 12 and 13. Does not include the practice squad or injured reserve. See
3.1, 3.2.

**Award-and-oblige** — A limit that a waiver or free agency award may cross, up to
a stated ceiling, leaving the team owing a corrective move. See 3.6(e).

**Best Ball** — The league's scoring format: each week the highest-scoring
eligible player at each position across the full Active Roster counts
automatically. No lineup is set. See 8.1(b).

**Cap Charge** — A contract's cost against a team's Salary Ceiling for a season.
See 5.2(a).

**Cash Window** — A three-season period over which the Multi-Season Cash Floor is
measured. See 5.4(e).

**Compliance instant** — The weekly moment at which every roster must be
compliant: Thursday 00:00, or 16:00 Wednesday in a week whose first game is a
Wednesday. See 1.4(f).

**Dead money** — The remaining cost of a contract that comes due when a player is
cut or traded. See 5.18.

**Deion Rule** — The rule requiring a season's real salary to be at least as much
as that season's prorated signing bonus charge. See 5.21.

**Designated provider** — A source the commissioner names to supply a fact these
rules need: statistics, position, injury designation or prospect grades. See 1.13.

**EDFL Pro Bowl** — The published record of 48 selections a season, used to fix a
Fifth Year Option tier. Published once and never recomputed. See 5.9(g).

**Exemption (poaching)** — An owner's designation of up to two of his practice
squad players as not poachable. Stands until released or until the player leaves
the practice squad; visible to every owner. See 5.17(l).

**Fifth Year Option** — A team's right to add one fully guaranteed real season to
a Round 1 rookie contract at a tier price set by the player's EDFL performance.
See 5.9(e)–(g).

**Franchise tag / Transition tag** — A one-year tag holding a pending free agent
for one more season. See 5.13.

**Free agency window** — The 24-hour sealed period the first valid offer on an
uncontracted player opens. Highest PPV wins; the earliest offer breaks a tie. See
5.14.

**Hold (practice squad)** — An owner's election to keep an elevated practice
squad player on the Active Roster through the Tuesday automatic return until the
owner moves him down. Weeks still count under 3.3(i). See 3.3(d)(i).

**Injury designation** — The status reported by the injury authority. Four —
Injured Reserve, Out, Doubtful and PUP — mark a player injured and are the only
ones that let him hold an injured reserve place. See 3.4(b).

**June 1st designation** — An election, available March 1st to May 31st and
limited to two per team per league year, charging a cut's future-season bonus
prorations to the following season. Cuts from June 1st through February 20th
receive the same split automatically without consuming a designation. A trade is
never eligible and never consumes one. See 5.18(c), 7.1(e).

**League Fund** — Where compliance and poaching fines are paid. Distributed at the
end of the season. See 6.7.

**League Reset Period** — February 21st through the end of February. Rosters and
contracts are frozen. Active contracts end at its start.

**League year rollover** — The March 1st advance of the league year, at which
every contract whose last real season has passed expires and outstanding void
proration comes due. See 1.12.

**Lock (practice squad)** — The state in which a player may no longer be placed on
a practice squad for the remainder of a season. See 3.3(i).

**Owner Cash** — A team's spending resource for a season, separate from Cap
Charge. May be earned, penalised or purchased. See 5.2(b), 10.1.

**Poaching** — Signing a player off another team's practice squad through a
24-hour sealed window, straight onto the signing team's Active Roster. See 5.17.

**PPV (Player Perceived Value)** — One number expressing a contract's worth to the
player, used to compare contracts of different shapes. See 5.2(c)–(d).

**Practice squad** — Up to seven places, at most three of them holding non-rookies.
Costs full Owner Cash; only guaranteed money and bonuses count against the cap
while a player sits there. See 3.3, 5.16.

**Proration** — Spreading a bonus's Cap Charge evenly across the seasons of a
contract, void seasons included. See 5.10(b).

**Restructure** — Converting salary still owed this season into a new signing
bonus paid now and prorated over two to five seasons. See 5.19.

**Roster bonus** — A lump sum that becomes a real Cap Charge on a fixed date
within its own season rather than prorating across years. The conversion date is
September 2nd.

**Rookie Wage Scale** — The pick-by-pick reference table that sets every rookie's
contract without negotiation. See 5.9.

**Rumour desk** — The league's publication route for owner-submitted claims about
players, picks and prospects, at three attribution tiers. A submission need not be
true. See 7.9(c).

**Salary Ceiling** — A team's individual cap for a season: the league base cap plus
that team's rollover from the immediately preceding season. See 5.5.

**Salary floors** — Two separate minimums. The **Season Cap Floor** is 89% of the
base cap, measured in Cap, tested every season. The **Multi-Season Cash Floor** is
89% of the summed base caps across a three-season Cash Window, measured in Owner
Cash actually paid, tested at the end of the window. See 5.4.

**Self-claim** — A waiver claim by the team that waived the player. It resets his
practice squad count and lifts any lock. See 5.15(n), 3.3(i)(v).

**Signing bonus** — Money paid to the player immediately at signing, then prorated
across the cap for every season of the deal. See 5.10(b).

**Trade block** — An owner's advisory, non-binding mark that a player he holds is
available. 14 days, unless watchlist interest sustains it. See 7.9(a).

**Void season** — A contract season with no real salary, existing to hold bonus
proration. Three kinds: **owner-elected**, added at signing to spread a signing
bonus; **option-bonus**, added automatically when an option bonus is scheduled;
and **restructure**, added automatically where a restructure's proration runs past
the contract's end. Owner-elected void seasons never take a contract past five
seasons; the other two can. Proration in a void season is never forgiven. See
5.7, 5.10(c), 5.19(e).

**Waivers** — The in-season wire every cut player passes through before he is
released or signed elsewhere. Opens Tuesday 00:00, runs Wednesday 00:00; priority
is points for, lowest first, and is not consumed by winning. See 5.15.

**Watchlist** — An owner's mark of interest in a player he does not hold, at a
visibility tier of his choosing. Private interest is unattributed, not hidden, and
is never read by the rumour desk. See 7.9(b), (d).

**Week (league week)** — One of the fourteen regular-season game weeks, running on
the four instants in 1.4(f). A week is final four hours after its last kickoff.

# APPENDIX A — OFFICER AUTHORITY

This appendix states what an officer may do that an owner may not. It is part of
these rules.

## A.1 Correction and reversal powers

Each power below is exercised with a stated reason, published under 1.11, and
leaves the corrected action on the public record marked as corrected or reversed.

(a) **Cut reversal.** Within **96 hours** of a cut, the commissioner may reverse
it, subject to four conditions, none waivable: the cut is in the current season;
the player has not signed elsewhere; no auction tier has been verified since the
cut; and the 96-hour window is open.

(b) **Cut from any roster.** The commissioner may cut a player from any team's
roster, with all normal cap and cash consequences under 5.18. This is the
mechanism behind the compliance action in 3.6(a) and (b)(i).

(c) **Deletion.** Deleting a record is for correcting a mistake only, never a
substitute for a cut. See 1.11(b).

(d) **Owner Cash adjustment.** Recorded as an individual ledger transaction under
10.1(b).

(e) **Trade reversal.** Under 7.7(h), within 96 hours of execution, subject to the
five conditions in 7.7(h)(iii).

(f) **Restructure reversal.** Within 96 hours, under 5.19(k). Available to the
commissioner or co-commissioner.

(g) **League year rollover, and its reversal.** Under 1.12(a) and 1.12(f).

(h) **Fifth Year Option reversal.** Within 96 hours of the decision, under 5.9(e).
Available to the commissioner or co-commissioner.

(i) **Resolution of a free agency or poach window.** Under 5.14(h) and 5.17(b).
Available to the commissioner or co-commissioner.

(j) **Acting for a team.** An officer may exercise on a team's behalf any decision
these rules give to that team where the team is on auto-drive under 2.1(b)(ii),
or where 5.9(e) or 5.19(b) expressly permits it.

## A.2 Powers reserved to the commissioner

The co-commissioner holds every officer power except the following, which are the
commissioner's alone:

(a) Publishing a Player Value Chart snapshot, and mapping a chart name to a
player.

(b) Viewing unpublished chart snapshots and their values.

(c) Granting or revoking commissioner or co-commissioner status.

(d) Disapproving a trade for competitive balance under 7.7(d).

(e) Setting or altering a League Calendar entry under 1.5(d).

(f) Appointing or removing an officer under 1.2(f).

## A.3 Recusal

(a) 7.7(e) applies to the co-commissioner exactly as to the commissioner. Neither
may approve or execute a trade involving his own team.

(b) Where both are conflicted, approval routes to the approvers under 1.2(d).

(c) **Reversal is deliberately narrower.** The co-commissioner may not reverse a
trade involving his own team; the commissioner may reverse one involving his.
Approval is a judgement that a trade ought to stand, which no owner can make about
his own team. A reversal withdraws a decision already taken, most often the
approver's own error, and a commissioner who has approved a trade wrongly must
retain the means to take it back.

## A.4 Transparency

Every power in this appendix is written to the public Action Log under 1.11,
including the appointment and removal of an officer.
